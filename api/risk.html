WORKED EXAMPLE / EURUSD

A $100 budget.
A $99 modeled loss.

For a $10,000 account risking 1%, the budget is $100. The distance from 1.0850 to 1.0820 is 30 pips. At $10 per pip per standard lot, the raw size is one-third of a lot.

Flooring to a 0.01-lot step gives 0.33 lots, or 33,000 units. The modeled stop loss is $99.

Example assumes 100,000 base units per lot, USD account and quote currency, and no execution costs. This is a fixture, not a broker availability claim.

POST /v1/risk/position-size · beta
{
  "account_balance": "10000.00",
  "risk_percent": "1.0",
  "entry_price": "1.0850",
  "stop_loss_price": "1.0820",
  "symbol": "EURUSD",
  "instrument_type": "forex",
  "contract_size": "100000",
  "quote_to_account_rate": "1",
  "lot_step": "0.01",
  "minimum_lot": "0.01",
  "account_currency": "USD",
  "direction": "long"
}

Decimals in. Decimals out.

Money, prices and quantities use decimal strings so the client does not introduce floating-point rounding.

Round size down.

Quantity steps are explicit. A model must never round a position above the supplied risk budget.

Know the assumptions.

Conversion rates, contract size, fees and entry basis matter. Unknown instrument metadata must not be guessed.

RELEASE BOUNDARY

Focused on Forex first.

Futures, stocks, crypto and broker-specific CFD models are later work. Liquidation estimates and options payoffs are outside the initial release.

Simple margin is a notional/leverage calculation. Actual broker margin, slippage, gaps and fees can differ from these examples.
