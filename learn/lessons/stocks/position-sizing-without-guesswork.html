Position Sizing Without Guesswork

How to size by stop distance and fixed account risk.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Separate risk from buying power

Position sizing starts with the loss budget in your hypothetical plan, not the largest order a broker permits. Divide that budget by the distance between entry and the planned stop. For ordinary shares, that distance is the price risk per share before costs. Then compare the resulting purchase value with available cash or permitted margin.

Allow for imperfect execution

Whole-share rules require rounding down. Fees and possible slippage consume part of the budget, while an overnight gap can make the actual loss larger than the stop-distance calculation. If a logical stop requires a position smaller than the minimum available size, the trade may not fit the plan. Widening the loss budget merely to keep the trade defeats the calculation.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A $100 planned loss and a $2.50 stop distance give 40 shares before costs. At a $60 entry the position value is $2,400. Risk budget and capital required are different numbers.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Use a worksheet to compare two stop distances with the same risk budget. Divide the budget by each stop distance and explain why doubling the distance halves the calculated share quantity.
