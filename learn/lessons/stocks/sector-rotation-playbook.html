Sector Rotation Playbook

Use relative strength to focus on leaders and avoid laggards.

Measure relative performance consistently

Sector rotation describes changes in which industries lead or lag a broader market. Start with a benchmark, a common measurement window and comparable return data. A sector can outperform while both it and the benchmark fall; relative strength alone does not establish a positive absolute return.

Look through the sector label

Two sector funds can use different classification systems, weights and holdings. A large constituent can dominate a fund’s performance. Compare concentration, overlap, costs and earnings exposure before treating a sector as a single economic bet. Choosing the best performer after the period has finished creates hindsight bias; a research rule needs to specify the information available at the decision date.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

If a sector falls 2% while the benchmark falls 5%, its simple return difference is +3 percentage points. That is relative outperformance alongside an absolute loss.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Choose a fixed historical window and compare two sectors with one benchmark. Repeat for a different window and note whether the apparent leadership changes.
