How the market is organized

A listed share can trade on several venues even though it has a primary listing exchange. Quotes show a bid and an ask; the difference is the spread. Displayed size describes the quantity available at a quoted price, not a promise that all of your order will fill there. Regular sessions, opening auctions and extended hours can have very different liquidity.

Build an execution checklist

Before studying an entry, identify the ticker, listing venue, session and upcoming company events. Compare the spread with the price move your idea depends on. A market order prioritizes execution but can fill at several prices. A limit order sets a price constraint but may remain unfilled. Trading halts and overnight gaps can interrupt either plan.

Put the idea into numbers

Suppose a stock is quoted at $49.98 bid and $50.02 ask. Buying and immediately selling 100 shares at those prices costs $4 in spread alone, before commissions or price movement.

Try it in your research

Observe one liquid and one less liquid stock at the same time. Record bid, ask and displayed size. Explain which order would be more exposed to slippage and why.
