Distinguish a benchmark from a product

An index is a calculated measure built from a set of constituents and weighting rules. It is not itself the same as an ETF, futures contract or other product linked to it. Price-return and total-return versions differ because the latter accounts for distributions according to the methodology.

Read the concentration

A capitalization-weighted index can be driven by its largest constituents. Equal-weighted versions can behave differently even with similar membership. Check rebalancing rules, sector exposure and currency before using an index as a market proxy. A broad index rising does not mean that most individual securities are rising.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

In a two-company illustration, a company weighted 80% rises 5% while the company weighted 20% falls 5%. The weighted index rises about 3% even though one of its two members falls.

Try it in your research

Compare a price-weighted, capitalization-weighted and equal-weighted methodology. Explain what a large price move in one constituent would mean in each.
