Breadth Confirmation Checklist

A/D line, sector participation, and internals.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Measure participation

Market breadth asks how widely a move is shared. An advance-decline count, the proportion above a moving average and sector participation each measure different things. Define the security universe and avoid comparing a broad exchange statistic with a much narrower index as if their memberships were identical.

Treat divergences as context

An index can rise while participation narrows because its largest constituents have strong returns. That divergence may be useful context, but it does not specify the date of a reversal. Check changes in index membership and the handling of missing data before comparing history. Several breadth measures built from the same prices are not independent observations.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

An index rises while 40 constituents advance and 60 decline. The count signals narrow participation, but the index return also depends on the weights and size of each move.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

For one historical date, compare index return, advancing share and sector returns. Explain what each observation adds and what it cannot tell you about the next session.
