Keep volatility measures distinct

Implied volatility reflects option prices and a model; realized volatility describes observed returns. A volatility index is not a direct prediction of market direction. ATR measures price range in the units of the instrument and is different from annualized return volatility.

Compare like-for-like windows

A position plan may use volatility to explore different stop distances or exposure scenarios, but any sizing rule needs explicit assumptions. An increase in volatility can occur after losses have already happened. Historical calm does not justify ignoring event risk, and low volatility does not imply that a leveraged position is safe.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

If an illustrative ATR doubles from 10 to 20 points, a fixed two-ATR stop doubles from 20 to 40 points. Keeping the same loss budget would halve a simple linear position size before rounding and costs.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Compare an implied measure and a realized measure over the same event. State their units, lookback or horizon, and why their readings need not agree.
