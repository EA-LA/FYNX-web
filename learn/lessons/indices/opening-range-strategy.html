Opening Range Strategy

Using first 30 minutes to frame risk and direction.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Fix the definition of the range

An opening range is the high and low over a chosen interval after a specified opening time. It might use the cash-market open or another session boundary. The interval, data source and treatment of premarket activity must be defined before testing a breakout idea.

Measure failures as carefully as successes

A move outside the range can reverse immediately. Record entry rules, invalidation, slippage and scheduled releases rather than choosing only clean examples. A wider opening range can imply a larger stop distance, which changes the quantity allowed by a fixed loss budget. The range is an observation, not proof of an edge.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

If the first 30 minutes span 100 to 102, a later trade at 102.10 is above the observed range. Whether it qualifies as a signal depends on a prewritten rule, such as a closing price rather than an intrabar touch.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Compare touch-based and close-based rules on the same historical sessions. Include false breakouts and costs without changing the rule after seeing results.
