Futures Margin and Leverage 101

Why leverage helps and hurts faster than most expect.

Margin is a performance bond

Futures margin supports the obligations of a leveraged contract; it is not the full purchase price of the underlying exposure. Initial and maintenance requirements can differ, and a broker may impose additional or intraday requirements. Changes in market volatility can lead to higher requirements.

Stress the account, not only the entry

Notional exposure comes from contract price, multiplier and quantity. Profit and loss can be large relative to the margin posted. Compare an adverse price scenario with account equity, cash needs and other positions. A low intraday margin offer does not make the position economically small, and losses can exceed the amount initially deposited.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A hypothetical $100,000 notional position supported by $5,000 of margin has 20 times as much notional as posted margin. A 2% adverse move represents roughly $2,000 for a linear exposure before costs.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

For a paper position, list notional exposure, initial margin, maintenance margin and a stressed loss separately. Explain which number changes when the broker raises its requirement.
