Read the contract specification first

A futures contract specifies an underlying exposure, multiplier, minimum price increment, expiration and settlement process. Contract symbols can look similar while representing different sizes. Cash-settled contracts and physically delivered contracts create different obligations as expiration approaches.

Convert price movement into currency

Tick value equals tick size multiplied by the contract multiplier for a simple linear contract. Planned risk then depends on ticks to the stop and the number of contracts. Check the exchange specification and your broker’s rules rather than inferring the multiplier from the symbol. Session breaks, price limits and rollover dates also affect execution.

Put the idea into numbers

A hypothetical contract with a 0.25-point tick and a $20-per-point multiplier has a $5 tick value. Eight ticks across three contracts represent $120 of price risk before fees and slippage.
