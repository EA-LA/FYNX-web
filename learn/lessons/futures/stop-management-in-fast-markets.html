Stop Management in Fast Markets

Design hard stop and invalidation logic for volatility spikes.

Distinguish an order from an outcome

A stop order is activated when its trigger condition is met; the execution that follows depends on order type, liquidity and venue rules. A stop-market order can slip. A stop-limit order constrains price but may fail to execute if the market moves through the limit.

Plan for discontinuous prices

Fast moves can widen spreads, consume displayed liquidity and trigger exchange limits. Compare your intended size with the contract’s tick value and plausible slippage. A research plan should state what happens if the position cannot be exited immediately. Increasing distance after entry changes the original loss budget unless size is reduced or the plan is reassessed.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A hypothetical stop triggers at 100 but fills at 98.5. With a $20-per-point multiplier and two contracts, that 1.5-point difference adds $60 of loss beyond the trigger-price estimate.
