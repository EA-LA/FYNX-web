Cross-Market Confirmation

Use bonds, dollar, and equities to validate futures ideas.

Explain the economic link

Cross-market research compares instruments that may respond to shared drivers, such as growth, inflation or funding conditions. A relationship needs an economic explanation and a defined time window. The same stock and bond markets can move together under one shock and in opposite directions under another.

Align data before drawing conclusions

Use synchronized timestamps, comparable returns and the correct contract month. A delayed quote or a rolling futures series can create a false divergence. Confirmation should be an additional observation, not an excuse to ignore the original invalidation level. Several correlated positions can also multiply one underlying risk rather than diversify it.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A long equity-index position and a short government-bond position may both be exposed to a growth narrative. A policy surprise can change both legs at once even though the tickers belong to different asset classes.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Choose a historical event and compare aligned returns across three markets. State the original hypothesis, an alternative explanation and a condition that would contradict it.
