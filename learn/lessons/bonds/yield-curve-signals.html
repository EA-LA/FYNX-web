Yield Curve Signals

Steepening, flattening, and recession probabilities.

Compare yields across maturities

A yield curve places yields from comparable instruments against their maturities. The slope between two points is a spread, often expressed in basis points. Steepening means that spread widens; flattening means it narrows. Either can happen while both yields rise or while both fall.

Avoid a one-variable forecast

Curve shapes reflect expectations, term premiums, supply and demand, among other influences. A historical relationship with growth does not give a precise timing signal. Compare like issuers and conventions, and identify which maturities are moving. A two-point spread leaves out changes in the middle of the curve.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A two-year yield of 4.0% and ten-year yield of 4.5% create a +50-basis-point spread. If the two-year rises to 4.4% while the ten-year stays at 4.5%, the spread flattens to +10 basis points.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Write three yield scenarios: a parallel shift, a steepening and a flattening. Identify how a short-duration and a long-duration holding could respond differently.
