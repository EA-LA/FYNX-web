Rates Cross-Asset Impact

How bond moves ripple into equities, FX, and gold.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Identify the reason yields moved

Higher yields can reflect stronger expected growth, higher inflation, increased supply or a changing risk premium. Those causes can have different implications for equities, currencies and gold. A chart of two prices moving together does not identify the driver by itself.

Compare exposures and timing

Rate-sensitive assets differ in cash flows, financing needs and currency exposure. A stock’s response depends on changes in expected earnings as well as discount rates. Currency returns involve the relative outlook between economies. Use aligned observation windows and separate an immediate reaction from a longer repricing.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A rise in yields driven by stronger growth can coincide with improving earnings expectations. A rise driven by inflation pressure without growth may produce a different equity response, even if the yield change is similar.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Compare two historical yield increases. For each, list evidence about growth, inflation and policy expectations, then describe why other asset returns differed.
