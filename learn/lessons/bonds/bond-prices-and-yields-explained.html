Bond Prices and Yields Explained

The inverse relationship and practical trading implications.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Discount future cash flows

A conventional fixed-rate bond promises specified coupon payments and principal at maturity, subject to the issuer meeting its obligations. Its market price reflects the discounted value of those cash flows. When the required yield rises, the present value generally falls; when the required yield falls, the price generally rises.

Distinguish yield measures

Coupon rate, current yield and yield to maturity answer different questions. Yield to maturity depends on contractual cash flows and assumptions about holding and reinvestment; it is not a guarantee of the realized return. Credit changes, embedded options, accrued interest and transaction costs can all matter alongside interest rates.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A hypothetical one-year zero-coupon bond paying $1,000 is worth about $952.38 at a 5% required yield: $1,000 ÷ 1.05. At 6%, the same payment is worth about $943.40.
