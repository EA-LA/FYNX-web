Use the correct duration concept

Modified duration estimates a bond’s percentage price sensitivity to a small change in yield, holding other assumptions constant. Macaulay duration measures a cash-flow-weighted time. Effective duration can account for changing cash flows in instruments with embedded options. These measures are related but are not interchangeable.

Know the approximation’s limits

For a small parallel yield change, approximate percentage price change is negative modified duration multiplied by the yield change in decimal form. Convexity becomes more relevant for larger moves, while credit spread changes and a reshaping yield curve add further effects. A duration figure alone does not describe default or liquidity risk.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A bond with modified duration 5 facing a 0.50 percentage-point yield increase has an approximate price change of −5 × 0.005 = −2.5%, before convexity and other changes.
