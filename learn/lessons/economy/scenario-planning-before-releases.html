Scenario Planning Before Releases

Predefine actions for hot/cold/inline outcomes.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •
Write conditions that can be checkedConnect scenarios to risk constraintsWorked examplePractice

Write conditions that can be checked

Scenario planning describes possible outcomes before an event. Define what “higher,” “lower” and “in line” mean in the units of the chosen data. Include a mixed-information scenario, since a headline can conflict with revisions or details. The purpose is to organize reasoning, not predict an exact price path.

Connect scenarios to risk constraints

For a paper plan, define the observation that would invalidate each interpretation, acceptable execution conditions and whether taking no action is an outcome. Avoid assigning probabilities without evidence. Afterward, compare what actually occurred with the original notes rather than rewriting the plan to match the result.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A headline inflation reading is higher than expected, but a closely watched underlying measure is lower. A plan with only “hot means sell” and “cool means buy” misses this mixed case.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Create a three-row scenario table for one upcoming release using hypothetical outcomes. Add an explicit “unclear information or poor liquidity” condition.
