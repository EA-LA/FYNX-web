Link central bank stance to risk-on/risk-off behavior.

Distinguish policy instruments and communication

A central bank can change a policy rate, adjust its balance sheet or communicate a future stance. These actions need not move together. Read the official decision and the stated conditions rather than inferring a complete policy cycle from one phrase or one market move.

Compare policy with expectations

Markets respond to changes in the expected path as well as the current setting. A cut can be less accommodative than investors expected, while an unchanged decision can carry more accommodative guidance. Economic conditions and the reason for the action matter. “Risk-on” and “risk-off” are broad descriptions, not rules that all assets must follow.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

If a hypothetical market expected a 50-basis-point cut and the bank delivers 25, the actual rate falls but the surprise is smaller easing than expected. These are two different descriptions of the same event.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Read an official policy statement and separate the action, economic assessment and forward conditions. Compare it with the previous statement without inventing a certainty about future decisions.
