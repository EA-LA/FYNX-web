Regime-Based ETF Rotation

Rotate between sectors based on macro regime.

Define the regime using available evidence

A market regime is a research label for conditions such as growth, inflation or volatility. The label is not directly observable in advance. Define the indicators, thresholds and publication lag before studying a rotation rule, and use only data that would have been available on each decision date.

Test costs and alternative explanations

A rotation backtest can look persuasive when it chooses favorable dates or uses revised economic data. Include turnover, spreads and fund expenses, then compare the result with a simpler benchmark. Examine losing periods and what happens when the regime classification is wrong. A macro narrative can explain a past return without providing a reliable timing rule.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

If a rule uses a monthly inflation release published in February, it cannot legitimately rebalance on January 31 using that future observation. The tradable decision date comes after publication.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Write a paper rotation rule with one indicator, a publication delay and a fixed review interval. Identify the transaction costs and revisions the test would need to handle.
