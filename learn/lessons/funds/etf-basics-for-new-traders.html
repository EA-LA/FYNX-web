Separate the fund from its trading price

An exchange-traded fund holds a portfolio or follows an investment mandate while its shares trade on an exchange. Net asset value reflects the value of the fund’s assets minus liabilities per share. The exchange price can differ from that estimate, particularly when underlying markets are closed or less liquid.

Read beyond the ticker

Inspect the objective, holdings, expense ratio, spread and the benchmark methodology. Two funds with similar names may provide different exposure. Tracking difference measures the return gap against a benchmark; tracking error describes variability in that gap. Neither is the same as the bid-ask spread paid when trading shares. Distributions also matter when comparing total returns.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

An ETF quoted at $100.10 against a $100.00 per-share NAV estimate trades at a 0.10% premium. Buying and later selling also involves the prevailing spread and any brokerage charges.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Compare the official factsheets of two funds with a similar objective. List one difference in holdings, costs and benchmark construction before comparing returns.
