Smart-Beta Fund Comparison

Factor exposure, overlap, and unintended concentration.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •
Compare the index rulesInspect unintended exposuresWorked examplePractice

Compare the index rules

Smart-beta funds apply a rules-based weighting scheme, such as value, quality, size or low volatility. The factor label is only a starting point. Inspect which metrics are used, how often the fund rebalances and whether sector or constituent limits constrain the result.

Inspect unintended exposures

A value screen can concentrate in particular industries, and a low-volatility screen can still experience substantial losses. Compare actual holdings, turnover, capacity, costs and overlap with existing positions. Historical factor premiums can disappear for long periods. A fund’s recent outperformance may reflect one sector or a few holdings rather than a durable factor advantage.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

Two funds called “quality” might use profitability alone or combine profitability with debt and earnings stability. They can therefore select different companies and react differently to changing financing conditions.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Read two index methodology summaries for the same factor label. Write the selection and weighting differences in plain language and identify one concentration risk.
