Give each timeframe a specific job

A multi-timeframe study separates broader context from execution detail. A daily chart might describe a range, an hourly chart a local structure, and a shorter chart the event used to test an entry hypothesis. The selected intervals should match the holding period and remain consistent through the study.

Avoid counting one move several times

A daily breakout and an hourly breakout may describe the same underlying price movement. They are not independent confirmations. Define what would invalidate the idea and measure the risk from the actual entry to that level. Looking at more timeframes until one agrees introduces selection bias and makes the rule difficult to evaluate.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A market can rise within an hourly range while remaining inside a larger daily downtrend. Both descriptions can be true; the intended holding period determines which hypothesis is being tested.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Annotate one historical chart at three fixed intervals. Write one observation per interval, then a single invalidation rule without changing the intervals after seeing the outcome.
