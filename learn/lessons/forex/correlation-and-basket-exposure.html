Correlation and Basket Exposure

Avoid hidden risk from correlated USD positions.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Map the currencies behind the tickers

Several currency pairs can share the same underlying exposure. Long EUR/USD and long GBP/USD both include a short-US-dollar component. Different pair names therefore do not establish diversification. Summing planned stop losses is useful, but it does not capture a common gap or a changing correlation.

Measure a relationship with care

Compare returns over aligned timestamps and use an appropriate quote direction. Reversing a currency pair changes the interpretation. Correlation is estimated from a chosen sample and can shift sharply around policy events. A risk review should include both the observed relationship and a stress scenario in which positions move against the account together.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

Two paper positions each budget $50 of stop risk, for $100 combined. A single dollar shock can affect both, while slippage can push actual losses beyond the combined estimate.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Create a currency exposure table for three pairs. Identify repeated long and short currencies, then compare it with a return-correlation matrix over more than one window.
