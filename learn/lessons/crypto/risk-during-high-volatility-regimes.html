Risk During High-Volatility Regimes

How to survive cascading liquidations and correlation shocks.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •
Consider a chain of stressed eventsWrite operational assumptions downWorked examplePractice

Consider a chain of stressed events

A crypto selloff can combine falling collateral value, higher margin pressure and thinner liquidity. Forced selling can amplify a move, but a liquidation chart does not predict exactly when that will occur. Correlations estimated during calm periods may understate the possibility of several holdings falling together.

Write operational assumptions down

A paper stress test should include price gaps, spread widening, unavailable exchange access and changes in collateral value. A stop order cannot protect against every venue outage. Account for where assets are held and whether positions share collateral. Reducing a position after a shock may be much more expensive than the pre-event quote suggests.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

If a $10,000 asset exposure falls 15%, the price loss is $1,500 before costs. If its collateral also falls in value, margin pressure can worsen beyond that simple calculation.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

Stress a hypothetical account with an asset decline, a collateral decline and a temporary inability to trade. Identify which assumptions a basic liquidation calculator cannot represent.
