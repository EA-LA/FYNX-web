Spot vs Perpetual Futures

Funding, liquidation, and leverage trade-offs.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Identify the exposure and obligations

A spot position generally involves holding an asset or a claim on it with a custodian. A perpetual derivative provides contract exposure without a fixed ordinary expiration, subject to the venue’s rules. Funding payments can flow between long and short holders, and collateral supports leveraged positions.

Check the margin model

Isolated and cross margin allocate collateral differently. Liquidation can use a mark price rather than the last traded price, and maintenance requirements may increase with position size. Positive funding does not guarantee that its sign or magnitude will persist. The product may also be unavailable or restricted in some jurisdictions.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A hypothetical $5,000 linear perpetual position with a 0.01% funding payment incurs $0.50 for that interval if the position is on the paying side. Repeated intervals and changing rates alter the total.
