IV Regimes and Strategy Selection

When to buy premium vs sell premium.

Implied volatility is embedded in price

Implied volatility is the model input consistent with an observed option price, given the other assumptions. It varies across strikes and expirations. Comparing its current level with history requires the same measure and a defined window; “high” relative to a quiet period need not mean an option is overpriced.

Match the full risk profile

Buying or selling premium creates exposure to direction, volatility, time and tail events. A post-event fall in implied volatility can offset a favorable underlying move for a long option. Selling premium can incur large losses when realized moves exceed expectations. Strategy labels do not establish suitability or positive expected returns.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A stock rises after earnings while a call loses value because the decline in implied volatility and time value outweighs the directional benefit. An expiration-only payoff chart cannot explain that change by itself.
