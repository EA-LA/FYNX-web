Reading the Greeks in Real Time

Delta, theta, vega and how they impact management.

FYNX learning guide • Educational example •

Greeks describe local sensitivities

Delta estimates sensitivity to the underlying, gamma describes how delta changes, theta relates to time passing, and vega relates to implied volatility. They are model estimates around the current state. Their units and signs depend on the provider and position direction, so check the convention before aggregating them.

Recalculate as conditions change

A large move can change the sensitivities themselves. Holding other inputs constant helps interpret one Greek but is rarely a complete description of a real session. A position may gain from direction and lose from volatility or time at the same time. Short and long positions invert exposures, while a portfolio combines them across contracts.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Put the idea into numbers

A long option with delta 0.40 has a first-order estimate of +0.40 per unit for a 1-unit underlying rise, holding other inputs fixed. Gamma and changes in volatility or time can make the actual price change different.

Illustrative assumptions; no live quote or promised outcome.

Try it in your research

For a hypothetical contract, list the sign and unit of each Greek. Explain why delta alone cannot estimate the full outcome of an earnings announcement.
