How portfolio stress test works

Put every position on the same basis

Enter account equity and one to thirty positions in a common account currency. Use a positive signed notional for a long exposure and a negative value for a short exposure. Each line contains a symbol and its signed value. Convert foreign-currency positions before entering them; the tool does not retrieve prices or exchange rates.

Distinguish gross from net exposure

Gross exposure adds absolute position values, while net exposure retains their signs. Gross leverage divides gross exposure by account equity. The same percentage price shock is applied to every row, and the row effects are summed to estimate scenario P/L and equity. An offsetting net figure can therefore hide substantial gross exposure and instrument-specific risks.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

Follow the inputs through.

With $10,000 equity, positions of +$6,000, +$4,000 and −$2,000 have $12,000 gross and $8,000 net exposure. A parallel −5% shock produces −$300, −$200 and +$100: −$400 overall, leaving $9,600 modeled equity.

Assumptions and limits

This linear parallel-shock model is not value at risk. It ignores correlations, different asset shocks, nonlinear option payoffs, fees, margin calls and liquidation. It does not estimate how likely the scenario is.
