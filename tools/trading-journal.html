How trading journal works

Record a trade with enough context

The Journal entry includes symbol, date, entry, stop, target, realized P/L, session and notes. Enter the actual closed-trade result in your account currency. The distance between entry and stop describes planned price risk; it cannot replace a realized result that includes what actually happened during execution. Use notes to record the original thesis and any deviation from the plan.

Review the history you have recorded

The Journal dashboard includes trade count, win rate, realized P/L, session summaries, streaks, a calendar and trade-by-trade review. Date-range controls let you examine the recorded period. The cumulative curve reflects entered results rather than a live broker account. A free account is required to save and synchronize entries; optional Pro products add separate advanced analysis and CSV export.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

Follow the inputs through.

Suppose a planned long entry is 100 with a stop at 98 and target at 104. The planned price ratio is 2:1. If the actual closed trade returned $75 after your chosen accounting treatment, record $75 as realized P/L rather than substituting the hypothetical target profit.

Assumptions and limits

The Journal relies on what you enter. It does not execute trades or automatically reconcile broker statements. Sample results shown in demo mode are examples, not your performance.
