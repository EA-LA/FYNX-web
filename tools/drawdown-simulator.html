How drawdown simulator works

Make the assumptions explicit

Enter starting equity, risk per trade, assumed win rate, reward-to-risk ratio and one to five hundred trades per path. Each simulated loss reduces current equity by the entered risk fraction; a win increases current equity by that fraction multiplied by the reward-to-risk assumption. Position risk therefore compounds as equity changes.

Read the distribution, not just one curve

The output summarizes ending-equity percentiles, the median, the 90th-percentile maximum drawdown and the percentage of paths ending below the starting balance. One illustrated curve is only one of the 500 paths. A fixed random seed makes comparisons repeatable. Changing assumptions explores the model’s sensitivity; it does not reveal the true probability of a future trading outcome.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

Follow the inputs through.

At 1% risk per trade, ten consecutive modeled losses reduce $10,000 to $10,000 × 0.99¹⁰, approximately $9,043.82. That is a 9.56% decline. The example shows compounding; it does not estimate the chance of such a streak.

Assumptions and limits

Trades are modeled as independent with fixed win rate and payoff. Fees, slippage, changing market conditions and liquidity constraints are excluded. A percentile from assumed inputs is not a confidence guarantee or forecast.
