How position risk planner works

Translate the contract into account currency

Start with account balance and the percentage allocated to this hypothetical trade. Enter entry, stop and target prices for the same instrument. The contract multiplier converts one price unit into value; the quote-to-account conversion rate converts that value into your account currency. Verify both against the contract you intend to study. A share, a futures contract and a forex lot are different units.

Check size and execution together

The model divides the risk budget by the absolute entry-to-stop distance, multiplier and conversion rate. It rounds calculated units down to six decimal places and reports modeled loss at the stop, profit at the target and reward-to-risk ratio. The stop and target must lie on opposite sides of entry. A broker may require a larger size increment, so compare the output with permitted order sizes.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

Follow the inputs through.

For a hypothetical $10,000 balance, 1% risk, entry 100, stop 98 and target 104, with multiplier and conversion rate both 1, the budget is $100. The model gives 50 units, $100 of stop-distance loss and $200 of target-distance profit.

Assumptions and limits

Fees, gaps, slippage, margin requirements and broker size increments are excluded. A stop price is not a guaranteed execution price. The calculation does not decide whether the underlying trade is suitable.
